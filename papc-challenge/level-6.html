Theory, on paperPrograms that compute tables

Why the tracks meet here

The programs track built operators on lists, and checked them on examples. The proofs track proved laws about tables, for every table. Neither says that the programs are right: that \(\mathit{select}\) on a list computes \(\sigma\) on the table it represents. This level proves it, once for each operator. Then every law of the proofs track holds for the programs, with no new proof.

The programs compute the operators

Definition 6.1

The table of a list \(l\) is \(\mathit{table}(l) = \{t \mid t \text{ is in } l\}\): the one \(l\) represents (Definition 1.2). A condition \(p\) with values in \(\B\) gives the property "\(p(t) = \true\)".

Theorem 6.2

For all lists \(l\) and \(m\):

\[\begin{aligned} \mathit{table}(\mathit{select}\ p\ l) &= \sel{p}(\mathit{table}(l)) & \mathit{table}(l \cat m) &= \mathit{table}(l) \cup \mathit{table}(m) \\ \mathit{table}(\mathit{project}\ f\ l) &= f[\mathit{table}(l)] & \mathit{table}(\mathit{product}\ l\ m) &= \mathit{table}(l) \times \mathit{table}(m) \end{aligned}\]

On the left, the programs of Levels 2F and 3F; on the right, the operators of Levels 2T to 4T. As a picture: going down by the program and then across to the table, or across first and then down by the operator, gives the same table.

\[\begin{array}{ccc} l & \xrightarrow{\ \mathit{table}\ } & \mathit{table}(l) \\ {\scriptstyle \mathit{select}\ p}\big\downarrow & & \big\downarrow{\scriptstyle \sigma_p} \\ \mathit{select}\ p\ l & \xrightarrow{\ \mathit{table}\ } & \sel{p}(\mathit{table}(l)) \end{array}\]

One induction, and every law comes for free

Proof of Theorem 6.2, for select. By induction on \(l\). For \([\,]\), both sides are empty. For \(t :: l\), if \(p(t) = \true\), the table of \(t :: \mathit{select}\ p\ l\) is \(\{t\} \cup \sel{p}(\mathit{table}(l))\), by the induction hypothesis, which is \(\sel{p}(\{t\} \cup \mathit{table}(l))\). If \(p(t) = \false\), \(t\) is dropped on both sides. ∎

Corollary 6.3

Every law of Levels 2T to 4T holds for the tables of lists. For example: \(\mathit{table}(\mathit{select}\ p\ (\mathit{select}\ q\ l)) = \mathit{table}(\mathit{select}\ (p \wedge q)\ l)\).

Proof. By Theorem 6.2 twice, both sides are \(\sel{p}(\sel{q}(\mathit{table}(l)))\) and \(\sel{p \wedge q}(\mathit{table}(l))\), equal by law 1. ∎

Compare with Fact 5T.4, which proved law 1 for lists by a new induction. Corollary 6.3 needs none: Theorem 6.2 does the work once, for every law at the same time.

Programs that compare rows

\(\mathit{diff}\) and \(\mathit{dedup}\) (Level 4F) compare rows with an equality test. They compute the right tables only if the test is right:

Theorem 6.4

If \(\mathit{eq}\) is an equality test (Definition 4F.1), then \(\mathit{table}(\mathit{diff}\ l\ m) = \mathit{table}(l) - \mathit{table}(m)\) and \(\mathit{table}(\mathit{dedup}\ l) = \mathit{table}(l)\).

In Lean, "== is an equality test" is the class LawfulBEq: it holds for Nat and String, and for every type whose == comes from DecidableEq.

Questions

Question 6.A Union

Prove Theorem 6.2 for \(\cat\).

Answer

\(t \in \mathit{table}(l \cat m) \iff t\) is in \(l \cat m\) \(\iff t\) is in \(l\) or \(t\) is in \(m\) \(\iff t \in \mathit{table}(l) \cup \mathit{table}(m)\). No induction: the middle step is a fact about appending.

Question 6.B Projection

Prove Theorem 6.2 for \(\mathit{project}\).

Answer

By induction on \(l\). For \([\,]\), both sides are empty. For \(t :: l\): \(\mathit{table}(f(t) :: \mathit{project}\ f\ l) = \{f(t)\} \cup f[\mathit{table}(l)]\) by the induction hypothesis, and \(f[\{t\} \cup \mathit{table}(l)] = \{f(t)\} \cup f[\mathit{table}(l)]\) by Fact 4T.5.

Question 6.C A broken equality test

With the broken test of Question 4F.D, find \(l\) and \(m\) with \(\mathit{table}(\mathit{diff}\ l\ m) \neq \mathit{table}(l) - \mathit{table}(m)\).

Answer

\(l = [1]\) and \(m = [2]\): the broken test finds \(1\) "in" \(m\), so \(\mathit{diff}\ l\ m = [\,]\), but \(\{1\} - \{2\} = \{1\}\).

Question 6.D Law 3 for lists, for free

Show \(\mathit{table}(\mathit{select}\ p\ (l \cat m)) = \mathit{table}(\mathit{select}\ p\ l \cat \mathit{select}\ p\ m)\) without induction.

Answer

By Theorem 6.2, the left side is \(\sel{p}(\mathit{table}(l) \cup \mathit{table}(m))\) and the right side is \(\sel{p}(\mathit{table}(l)) \cup \sel{p}(\mathit{table}(m))\). They are equal by law 3.

Lean, on your computerThe two tracks, joined

One line joins the two tracks

Leantotable.lean
-- The table a list represents (Definition 1.2)
def toTable {α : Type} (l : List α) : Sets.Table α :=
  fun t => t ∈ l

-- Union is appending: your union (3F) computes Sets.union (2T)
theorem toTable_union {α : Type} (l m : List α) (t : α) :
    toTable (union l m) t ↔ Sets.union (toTable l) (toTable m) t := by
  simp [toTable, union, Sets.union]
  • One file now holds both tracks: your programs (select, union, …) and Sets (Sets.select, …). If you did only one track, copy the other's definitions from Level 5T and Level 2T to Level 4T.
  • simp knows that t ∈ l ++ m means t ∈ l ∨ t ∈ m, and finishes (TPIL 5.7 Using the Simplifier).

Your turn: select computes a selection

Leanrepresent.lean
-- select computes Sets.select, with the condition "p r = true"
theorem toTable_select {α : Type} (p : α → Bool) (l : List α) (t : α) :
    toTable (select p l) t ↔
      Sets.select (fun r => p r = true) (toTable l) t := by
  sorry

Exercise 6.1 In Lean Theorem 6.2 for select

Induction on l; in the cons case, cases h : p row. Unfold with simp [toTable, select, Sets.select, h] at ih ⊢, then finish with ih and logic (Level 2T).

Hint

at ih ⊢ simplifies the induction hypothesis and the goal together, so that they match. If a goal remains, read it in the Infoview: it is usually "t = row or …", and ih covers the "…".

Your turn: a law for lists, with no new induction

Leanlaw.lean
-- Law 1 for lists, as tables (Corollary 6.3): no new induction
theorem toTable_select_select {α : Type} (p q : α → Bool)
    (l : List α) (t : α) :
    toTable (select p (select q l)) t ↔
      toTable (select (fun r => p r && q r) l) t := by
  sorry

Exercise 6.4 In Lean Corollary 6.3

Start with simp only [toTable_select, Sets.select], then finish with logic, or with your Sets.select_select.
