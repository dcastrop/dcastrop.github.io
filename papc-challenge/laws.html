The laws

LawHolds whenProved
1\(\sel{p}(\sel{q}(R)) = \sel{p \wedge q}(R)\)always2T; for lists 5T, 6
2\(\sel{p}(\sel{q}(R)) = \sel{q}(\sel{p}(R))\)always2T
3\(\sel{p}(R \cup S) = \sel{p}(R) \cup \sel{p}(S)\)always2T
4\(\sel{p}(R - S) = \sel{p}(R) - S\)always3T
5\(\sel{p}(R \times S) = \sel{p}(R) \times S\)\(p\) mentions only \(R\)4T; for queries 7
6\(\sel{p}(R \join S) = \sel{p}(R) \join S\)\(p\) mentions only \(R\)7
7\(\proj{A}(\proj{B}(R)) = \proj{A}(R)\)\(A \subseteq B\)7
8\(\proj{A}(R \cup S) = \proj{A}(R) \cup \proj{A}(S)\)always7; images 4T
9\(\proj{A}(\sel{p}(R)) = \sel{p}(\proj{A}(R))\)\(p\) mentions only \(A\)7
10\(R \join S = S \join R\)always7
11\((R \join S) \join T = R \join (S \join T)\)always7

A side condition is part of the law: without it, the two sides differ, or one of them makes no sense (Exercise 4T.3, Question 7.B).

Looks like a lawButSee

\(\pi_1[R \times S] = R\)only when \(S\) is not emptyQuestion 4T.A
