## Our challenge: teach Lean to answer questions about tables

Here are two tables of our club: who is a member, in which department; and who came to which weekly session.

@club Member Attended

::: note
Which CS members came in week 1? Alan.
:::

- To answer it, we keep some rows, keep some columns, and match two tables. This is **relational algebra**, the maths behind every relational database.
- The challenge is to write it in **Lean**, and prove, by hand, that it always gives the right answer.

In the language you will learn, the question is $\proj{\att{name}}(\sel{\att{dept} = \val{CS}}(\rel{Member}) \join \sel{\att{week} = 1}(\rel{Attended}))$. Do not worry if it means nothing to you yet: each symbol in it gets a level of its own, and [[1]] starts from what a table is.

## Why prove it? Databases rewrite your questions

- To answer fast, a database rewrites your question into a quicker one, which must have the same answer.
- Each rewrite follows a **law**, such as "filter first, then combine". Some laws hold only under a side condition.
- Get one wrong, and the database silently answers wrongly. Testing tools have found hundreds of such bugs in mature databases ([SQLancer](https://github.com/sqlancer/sqlancer); M. Rigger and Z. Su, [ESEC/FSE 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3368089.3409710)).

::: note
A law can be **proved**. In this challenge, you prove them.
:::

## Start at Level 1, then pick a track, or both {#levels}

Everyone starts at [[1]]. Then come two tracks, which you can take in either order, or side by side:

- **Programs first** follows [@fpil], chapter by chapter: you build the operators of relational algebra as Lean programs, and run them on the club's tables.
- **Proofs first** follows [@tpil], chapter by chapter: you prove the laws of relational algebra in Lean, for every table.

@map

The tracks meet at [[6]], which needs both: there, you prove that your programs compute exactly what the laws are about.

## What you will have built

1. **Your first proof**: Lean checks a fact about the club's tables ([[1]]).
2. **A query engine on lists**: you program selection, projection, product, union, difference and joins, and run them on the club's tables (Levels [[2f|2F]] to [[4f|4F]]).
3. **Laws for every table**: you prove laws 1 to 5 once, for all tables; law 5 needs a side condition (Levels [[2t|2T]] to [[4t|4T]]).
4. **A query language**: queries become terms, and `eval` answers them ([[5f]]). With **induction**, you prove facts about every list ([[5t]]).
5. **Programs proved correct**: your programs compute the operators, so every law holds for them ([[6]]).
6. **Laws for every query**: each side condition becomes a precise hypothesis about attribute names ([[7]]).
7. **A verified optimiser**: it rewrites queries and never changes an answer ([[8]]).
8. **A verified fast join**: it computes the same table as the definition, much faster ([[9]]).
