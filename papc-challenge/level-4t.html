Theory, on paperProducts, images, and a side condition

A product pairs every row with every row

Definition 4T.1 (Product)

For tables \(R\) and \(S\): \(\ R \times S = \{(t, s) \mid t \in R \text{ and } s \in S\}\).

Its rows are pairs of rows. For example, \(((\text{Ada}, \text{Maths}), (1, \text{Lists})) \in \rel{Member} \times \rel{Session}\), which has \(3 \cdot 4 = 12\) rows. In SQL: FROM Member, Session.

An image applies a function to every row

Definition 4T.2 (Image, projection)

For a function \(f\) on rows, \(f[R] = \{s \mid \text{there is } t \in R \text{ with } f(t) = s\}\). The projections are the images under \(\pi_1(d_1, d_2) = d_1\) and \(\pi_2(d_1, d_2) = d_2\).

For example, \(\pi_2[\rel{Attended}] = \{1, 2, 3\}\): the weeks someone attended. Read more in Abiteboul, Hull, Vianu, Foundations of Databases (1995), ch. 4: Conjunctive Queries.

Membership in an image is a "there is": \(s \in f[R]\) holds when some row of \(R\) is sent to \(s\). To prove it, give the row (a witness); to use it, you get a row, but you do not choose which one.

"For all" and "there exists"

To proveIn LeanA proof is
for every \(x\), \(P(x)\)∀ x, P xa function fun x => … that proves P x for each x
there is \(x\) with \(P(x)\)∃ x, P xa pair ⟨w, h⟩ of a witness w and a proof h of P w

"For all" is a function, like "if … then"; "there exists" is a pair, like "and". To use h : ∀ x, P x, apply it: h a proves P a. To use h : ∃ x, P x, take it apart: obtain ⟨w, hw⟩ := h (TPIL 4.1 The Universal Quantifier, TPIL 4.4 The Existential Quantifier).

Images compose

Fact 4T.3 (Composition)

\(g[f[R]] = (g \circ f)[R]\).

Proof. For every \(s\):

\[\begin{aligned} s \in g[f[R]] &\iff \text{there is } u \in f[R] \text{ with } s = g(u) \\ &\iff \text{there is } t \in R \text{ with } s = g(f(t)) \\ &\iff s \in (g \circ f)[R]. \end{aligned}\]

In the middle step: from left to right, take apart the two "there is"; from right to left, the witness for \(u\) is \(f(t)\). ∎

A side condition is a hypothesis of the law

Definition 4T.4 (Mentions only \(R\))

A condition \(p\) on the rows of \(R \times S\) mentions only \(R\) if there is a condition \(p'\) on the rows of \(R\) with: for all \(t\) and \(s\), \(p(t, s)\) holds exactly when \(p'(t)\) holds.

Law 5

If \(p\) mentions only \(R\), then \(\sel{p}(R \times S) = \sel{p'}(R) \times S\).

Proof. \((t, s) \in \sel{p}(R \times S) \iff t \in R\), \(s \in S\) and \(p(t, s)\) \(\iff t \in R\), \(p'(t)\) and \(s \in S\) \(\iff (t, s) \in \sel{p'}(R) \times S\). The middle step is the hypothesis, for this \(t\) and \(s\). ∎

Without the hypothesis, the law makes no sense: a condition on the week of a session cannot be checked on \(\rel{Member}\) alone. Law 5 is what lets a database filter a table before combining it with another, which is much faster (Abiteboul, Hull, Vianu, Foundations of Databases (1995), ch. 6: Static Analysis and Optimization, section 6.1). Level 7 states it for queries, where "mentions only \(R\)" becomes a fact about attribute names.

More facts about images

Fact 4T.5

\(f[R \cup S] = f[R] \cup f[S]\).

Fact 4T.6

\(f[R \cap S] \subseteq f[R] \cap f[S]\), and the two sides can differ.

Fact 4T.6 is a trap: it looks like Fact 4T.5, but only one inclusion holds. Question 4T.B finds two tables that separate the sides. Level 7 meets the same trap with projection and difference.

Questions

Question 4T.A Project a product

Is \(\pi_1[R \times S] = R\) for all tables \(R\) and \(S\)?

Answer

Only when \(S\) is not empty. If \(S = \emptyset\), then \(R \times S = \emptyset\), so \(\pi_1[R \times S] = \emptyset\), whatever \(R\) is. If \(S\) has a row \(s\), each \(t \in R\) is \(\pi_1(t, s)\).

Question 4T.B The trap

Find \(f\), \(R\) and \(S\) with \(f[R \cap S] \neq f[R] \cap f[S]\).

Answer

\(f = \pi_1\), \(R = \{(a, 1)\}\) and \(S = \{(a, 2)\}\). Then \(R \cap S = \emptyset\), so \(f[R \cap S] = \emptyset\), but \(f[R] \cap f[S] = \{a\}\).

Question 4T.C Prove Fact 4T.5

Prove \(f[R \cup S] = f[R] \cup f[S]\).

Answer

\(s \in f[R \cup S] \iff\) there is \(t\) with (\(t \in R\) or \(t \in S\)) and \(f(t) = s\) \(\iff\) (there is \(t \in R\) with \(f(t) = s\)) or (there is \(t \in S\) with \(f(t) = s\)) \(\iff s \in f[R] \cup f[S]\).

Lean, on your computerProducts, images, and tactics

Products and images are formulas, with "there exists"

Leanproduct.lean
namespace Sets
-- A row of the product is a pair x: x.1 a row of R, x.2 a row of S
def product {α β : Type} (R : Table α) (S : Table β) : Table (α × β) :=
  fun x => R x.1 ∧ S x.2

-- The image f[R]: the rows s with s = f t for some row t of R
def image {α β : Type} (f : α → β) (R : Table α) : Table β :=
  fun s => ∃ t, R t ∧ f t = s
end Sets

After your code of Levels 2T and 3T. ∃ t, R t ∧ f t = s says: there is a row t of R with f t = s. Type ∃ as \ex and ∀ as \all.

A first proof with tactics

Leanfirst.lean
-- Inside namespace Sets: each row t of R gives the row f t of f[R]
theorem image_of {α β : Type} (f : α → β) (R : Table α) (t : α)
    (h : R t) : image f R (f t) := by
  exact ⟨t, h, rfl⟩
  • by switches to tactics: commands that build the proof step by step. The Infoview shows what is left to prove after each one (TPIL 5.2 Basic Tactics).
  • exact gives the whole proof at once. ⟨t, h, rfl⟩ proves the ∃: t is the witness, h proves R t, and rfl proves f t = f t.
  • More tactics: intro h (assume, for → and ∀), constructor (split ∧ and ↔), obtain ⟨t, ht⟩ := h (take apart ∃ and ∧), apply.

Exercise 4T.1 In Lean Images compose

Prove Fact 4T.3. Start with constructor, then intro h in each half.

Hint 1

To use h : ∃ t, A t: obtain ⟨t, ht⟩ := h names the witness and its proof. Patterns can nest: obtain ⟨v, ⟨t, ht, hv⟩, hu⟩ := h.

Hint 2

From left to right, the witness is t; from right to left, you need two witnesses: f t for the outer image, and t for the inner one.

Hint 3

In a pattern, rfl in place of a proof of x = e replaces x by e everywhere. After obtain ⟨v, ⟨t, ht, rfl⟩, rfl⟩ := h, the goal mentions only t.

Your turn: law 5, side condition included

Leanlaw5.lean
-- Inside namespace Sets: law 5, with its side condition h
theorem select_product {α β : Type} (p : α × β → Prop) (p' : α → Prop)
    (h : ∀ t s, p (t, s) ↔ p' t) (R : Table α) (S : Table β)
    (t : α) (s : β) :
    select p (product R S) (t, s) ↔ product (select p' R) S (t, s) := by
  sorry

The hypothesis h is Definition 4T.4: it says that p mentions only R, with p' its condition on the rows of R.

Exercise 4T.3 On paper Why the hypothesis?

On paper: find \(p\), \(R\) and \(S\) that show why law 5 needs its hypothesis.

Answer

Take \(R = \rel{Member}\), \(S = \rel{Session}\), and \(p((n, d), (w, x))\): "\(w = 1\)". For a \(p'\) to exist, \(p'(\text{Ada}, \text{Maths})\) would have to hold, since \(p\) holds for \(((\text{Ada}, \text{Maths}), (1, \text{Lists}))\), and also not hold, since \(p\) fails for \(((\text{Ada}, \text{Maths}), (2, \text{Proofs}))\). No such \(p'\) exists: the right side of law 5 cannot even be written.
