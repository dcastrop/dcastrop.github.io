Our challenge: teach Lean to answer questions about tables

Here are two tables of our club: who is a member, in which department; and who came to which weekly session.

\(\rel{Member}\)
\(\att{name}\)\(\att{dept}\)
AdaMaths
AlanCS
GraceCS
\(\rel{Attended}\)
\(\att{name}\)\(\att{week}\)
Ada1
Alan1
Alan2
Grace3

Which CS members came in week 1? Alan.

  • To answer, keep some rows, keep some columns, and match two tables: relational algebra, the maths inside every database.
  • The challenge: write it in Lean, and prove, by hand, that it always gives the right answer.

In the language you will learn, the question is \(\proj{\att{name}}(\sel{\att{dept} = \val{CS}}(\rel{Member}) \join \sel{\att{week} = 1}(\rel{Attended}))\). If that means nothing yet, good: every symbol in it gets a level of its own, and Level 1 starts from what a table is.

Why prove it? Databases rewrite your questions

  • To answer fast, a database rewrites your question into a quicker one, which must have the same answer.
  • Each rewrite follows a law, such as "filter first, then combine". Some laws hold only under a side condition.
  • Get one wrong, and the database silently answers wrongly. Testing tools have found hundreds of such bugs in mature databases (SQLancer; M. Rigger and Z. Su, ESEC/FSE 2020).

A law can be proved. In this challenge, you prove them.

Start at Level 1, then pick a track, or both

Everyone starts at Level 1. Then two tracks, which you can take in either order, or side by side:

  • Programs first follows Functional Programming in Lean (FPIL), chapter by chapter: you build the operators of relational algebra as Lean programs, and run them on the club's tables.
  • Proofs first follows Theorem Proving in Lean 4 (TPIL), chapter by chapter: you prove the laws of relational algebra in Lean, for every table.

The tracks meet at Level 6, which needs both: there, you prove that your programs compute exactly what the laws are about. After it, Level 7 then Level 8, and Level 9, in either order.

Each level is a page of short lecture notes: what you build and prove, the book sections to read first, a little theory on paper (with questions and their answers), then Lean on your computer (with exercises and hints), and milestones to tick. Each level also exists as slides, in PDF.

What you will have built

  1. Your first proof, one word long: Lean checks a fact about the club's tables (Level 1).
  2. A query engine on lists: selection, projection, product, union, difference and joins, run on the club's tables (Levels 2F to 4F).
  3. Laws for every table: laws 1 to 5, proved once for all tables, including a side condition (Levels 2T to 4T).
  4. A query language: queries as terms, and eval, which answers them (Level 5F); and induction, which proves facts about every list (Level 5T).
  5. Programs proved correct: your programs compute the operators, so every law holds for them (Level 6).
  6. Laws for every query, each side condition a checkable hypothesis (Level 7).
  7. A verified optimiser, which rewrites queries and never changes an answer (Level 8).
  8. A verified fast join, which computes the same table as the definition, much faster (Level 9).
